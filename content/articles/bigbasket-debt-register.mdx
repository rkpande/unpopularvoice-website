BigBasket lost around ₹3,857 Cr across its two operating entities in FY25.

In the same year, no bank granted it a single new secured facility. No NBFC did either. The last new secured charge registered against either BigBasket entity was in October 2021, five years ago, for ₹5 Cr from HDFC Bank. And even that one is now satisfied.

Read the P&L and the register side by side. That combination is not what an Indian consumer-tech company of this size and this loss trajectory usually looks like.

## March 2017

The earliest active BigBasket-related secured facility on record is a ₹45 Cr charge registered against Innovative Retail Concepts Pvt Ltd on 29 March 2017. The counterparty is listed as "Others" in the register's classification, meaning a private lender rather than a scheduled commercial bank.

BigBasket had just raised its Series E round. It was still an independent standalone business, four years from being acquired by Tata Digital, and the pre-Tata capital structure still included some non-bank secured borrowing.

## December 2017

Two more facilities appear that year.

On 14 December 2017, a ₹35 Cr charge is registered against Supermarket Grocery Supplies Pvt Ltd. Counterparty: Others.

On 18 December 2017, a ₹25 Cr charge is registered against Innovative Retail Concepts Pvt Ltd. Counterparty: Others.

By end of calendar 2017, three registered secured facilities sit across the two BigBasket entities, totalling ₹105 Cr. All of them are with unnamed private lenders. No bank facility has been registered yet.

## July 2019

The single largest facility in the BigBasket charge history is registered on 26 July 2019. Amount: ₹100 Cr. Entity: Supermarket Grocery Supplies Pvt Ltd. Counterparty: Others.

This is roughly the peak of the pre-Tata secured-lending footprint. The total registered exposure across the two entities now stands at ₹205 Cr.

## October 2021

The one and only entry against a named bank appears on 26 October 2021.

A ₹5 Cr charge is registered against Innovative Retail Concepts Pvt Ltd. Charge holder: HDFC Bank Limited.

It arrives after the Tata Digital acquisition of BigBasket, which was announced in March 2021 and closed in the middle months of that year. A small, named-bank facility appears within months of the change of ownership. It is the only line on either entity's register where the counterparty is spelled out as a scheduled commercial bank.

## Everything after

Nothing. Not one new secured charge has been registered against either BigBasket entity since October 2021.

Five years of the current business cycle. Three complete financial years, plus FY26 in progress. No new secured lender relationships. No modifications to existing facilities that added principal. Zero additions to the register.

What did happen during that same five-year window is that every one of the five historical facilities was retired.

Total historical registered principal: ₹210 Cr. Currently active on record: ₹0.

## What the register does not carry, that other companies' registers do

An Indian consumer company at IRCPL and SGS's combined scale, posting the combined FY25 loss they posted, would typically have several active bank facilities on the register at this point in the cycle.

Working-capital lines with a scheduled bank. Term loans secured against receivables or inventory. NBFC facilities that get renewed each year. The register would show the mechanics of running a large operational business with normal debt-cycle behaviour.

For BigBasket, none of that appears. There is no working-capital line drawn against either entity. No NBFC facility is currently open. No term loan is pending. Through FY25, when the two entities together booked a reported loss of around ₹3,857 Cr, no secured lender granted new principal against either entity, and no existing facility was expanded.

## What the register says by omission

Two things.

The first is direct. The combined FY25 loss on the two BigBasket entities was absorbed by equity, not debt. No secured facility was drawn down in the year the losses widened at both. No new lender relationship was established at either. What absorbed the ₹3,857 Cr combined shortfall is Tata Digital's paid-up capital deployed onto the two entities' balance sheets, most visibly the ₹2,859.34 Cr on IRCPL.

The second is structural. This is not the funding profile of a normal Indian consumer-tech operator at this scale. This is the specific shape of a Tata-parent-funded model.

There is no rotating set of bank credit lines to renew each year. There is not a network of NBFC and private-credit lenders providing secured facilities in exchange for security over receivables. There is a single parent providing capital as equity, absorbing the loss on its own balance sheet, and not exposing either operating entity to servicing obligations.

The trade-off is not on the P&L. The trade-off is that in a normal funding structure, a combined ₹3,857 Cr loss at two operating entities would raise a serious question about the durability of the business model. In this funding structure, the question that replaces it is a different one: how long the parent stays willing to keep funding the loss in equity, and whether the FY26 audit shows a business that has stabilised enough to earn that willingness.

## One line to remember

Through the FY23-to-FY25 loss cycle, BigBasket did not add a single new secured facility across either of its two operating entities. In the year both entities recorded their widest losses on standalone record, the charges register did not move. That is what the Tata Digital funding model looks like on paper.
