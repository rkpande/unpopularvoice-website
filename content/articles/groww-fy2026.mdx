Groww is India's largest broker by its share of NSE active clients. Its first annual results as a listed company show it is also becoming a lender to the same people.

## Is Groww's profit cash?

Cash from operations was -₹20.60 Cr, against a profit of ₹2,083.00 Cr. The profit was earned in cash; then it was lent out.

- **Loans to customers absorbed ₹2,595.47 Cr.** For a lender, loans are operating activity, so a growing margin-funding book shows up here, not under investments. Loans on the balance sheet rose from ₹1,690.70 Cr to ₹4,247.16 Cr, and margin funding was ₹2,212.37 Cr of that rise.
- **Client money roughly netted out.** Money held for clients in bank accounts rose ₹3,013.58 Cr, and amounts owed to clients rose ₹3,400.01 Cr.
- **Tax paid was ₹732.30 Cr**, against ₹1,905.44 Cr in FY2025, which is part of why last year's cash from operations was a deeper -₹962.16 Cr.

None of it was funded with debt. Borrowings fell to ₹224.01 Cr after ₹417.47 Cr of repayments. Shares issued during the year brought in ₹2,736.64 Cr net of costs: ₹1,015.98 Cr net from the IPO's fresh issue (₹1,060 Cr gross), the rest mainly from a pre-IPO private placement and employee share allotments. Fisdom, the wealth platform bought in October 2025, cost ₹950.75 Cr in cash, net of cash acquired, and added ₹920.05 Cr of goodwill. Equity doubled to ₹9,651.36 Cr.

## Where does Groww's money come from?

Broking fees were ₹3,773.25 Cr, 81.2% of revenue, down from 85.5% a year earlier. The rest is interest, and it is the part that is growing.

Of every ₹100 of revenue, about ₹45 ended as profit.

- **Fees and commission: ₹3,773.25 Cr, up 13.1%.** Brokerage on stocks, derivatives and commodities.
- **Interest on margin funding: ₹257.66 Cr, up from ₹47.98 Cr.** Groww lends users part of the price of shares they buy. The audited accounts put that book at ₹2,814.26 Cr at year-end, against ₹601.89 Cr.
- **Interest on loans: ₹239.47 Cr, up 21.3%.** Mainly unsecured personal loans through its NBFC subsidiary, plus a new loans-against-securities book.
- **Interest on client funds: ₹374.20 Cr, up 16.9%.** Income on customer money and securities earmarked with exchanges.

Revenue grew by ₹742.86 Cr. Fees supplied 58.8% of that increase, lending 33.9% and client-fund interest 7.3%.

The listed company's own accounts show revenue of ₹3,059.03 Cr and profit of ₹1,629.74 Cr, a large part of that revenue charged to group companies for platform services. Consolidation eliminates those charges, so this piece uses the group figures throughout.

## Why did profit grow slower than revenue?

Strip out last year's one-off and profit grew about as fast as revenue. FY2025 staff costs were cut by a ₹106.17 Cr reversal of a cancelled management incentive plan. Taxed at the 25.17% statutory rate, that added roughly ₹79.45 Cr to FY2025 profit. On the remaining base of about ₹1,744.92 Cr, FY2026 profit grew about 19.4%, against revenue growth of 19.0%. This is approximate: the accounts do not state the tax effect of the reversal.

The reported margin still slipped, from 46.8% to 44.8%, and the cost lines split two ways.

**What held.** Marketing was ₹487.10 Cr, essentially unchanged from ₹487.58 Cr, and fell from 12.5% to 10.5% of revenue. Software, server and technology spend rose 4.2% to ₹459.37 Cr.

**What did not.**

- **Employee costs rose 87.5% to ₹590.83 Cr.** Without the reversal, 40.2%. Excluding also the ₹59.60 Cr the company attributes to Fisdom's staff, payroll still rose 26.1%, faster than revenue. Stock-based pay nearly doubled to ₹79.06 Cr.
- **Loan and receivable losses rose 95.6% to ₹140.06 Cr** (next section).
- **Depreciation and amortisation nearly doubled to ₹47.87 Cr**, including ₹14.25 Cr of amortisation on intangibles from the Fisdom purchase.

Marketing and technology gave operating leverage. Payroll did not, even excluding Fisdom, and credit costs on personal loans rose. The accounts report one segment, so they do not show how profitable lending or Fisdom is on its own.

Tax was ₹738.41 Cr, 26.1% of profit before tax and associates. Groww also chose not to book a ₹444.80 Cr tax asset on merger-related expenses and changes in the value of employee options, because the tax treatment is uncertain; if it is settled in Groww's favour, that would be a one-off gain, not operating profit.

## Where do Groww's credit losses come from?

About 40 paise of every rupee Groww earned in loan interest went to expected loan losses. The loan-loss charge was ₹96.34 Cr against ₹239.47 Cr of interest on loans, up from 34% in FY2025 (₹67.46 Cr against ₹197.49 Cr).

The audited notes split Groww's lending into two very different books:

- **Margin funding: ₹2,814.26 Cr, 4.7x last year.** Fully backed by shares and securities; any amount not covered by collateral is provided for in full. The loss allowance is nil in both years.
- **Unsecured loans, mainly personal: ₹1,292.71 Cr gross, up only 13.7%.** Write-offs were ₹81.04 Cr, against ₹32.97 Cr in FY2025. The loan-loss charge equals roughly 7.9% of the average book over the year.

The ₹140.06 Cr total in the profit and loss account also covers client receivables and other financial assets, which the notes do not fully itemise. Loans against securities, a new ₹203.25 Cr book, carry a ₹0.27 Cr allowance.

<SignatureLine text="Groww's fast-growing margin book booked no credit losses. Its slow-growing personal-loan book booked most of them." />

Rising write-offs can reflect a book that is ageing rather than one that is worsening. The management commentary describes "improved asset quality", and provisions on loans already in default did fall, to ₹11.56 Cr from ₹16.67 Cr. That came after ₹81.04 Cr of write-offs, 2.5x last year's. The accounts do not say which reading is right.

## How did Groww fare against Zerodha under the F&O rules?

Groww's fees grew 13.1% in FY2026, the first full year under the tighter derivatives rules that took effect from November 2024. Zerodha's revenue fell 11.5% in FY2025 as those rules arrived ([Zerodha FY2025](/zerodha-fy2025)).

The audited accounts do not split fees by product. The company's own operating data, which is not audited, points to four contributors:

- **Market share.** NSE active clients fell 7.1%, from 49.2 million to 45.7 million, per the annual report, while Groww says it held 28% of them in the March 2026 quarter. Growing while the market shrinks usually means taking share.
- **Derivatives.** Groww reports derivatives premium turnover per day up 48.6% and derivatives users up 21.4%. On its own data, its F&O activity grew under the rules.
- **Cash equities.** Stock turnover per day rose 24.9%, the company reports.
- **A late-year surge.** The March 2026 quarter brought ₹1,505.37 Cr of revenue, 43.9% above the ₹1,046.40 Cr average of the first nine months. Fisdom was consolidated in that quarter, so not all of the jump is trading.

The cost structures are not close. Groww spent 12.7% of revenue on employees, against 6.1% at Zerodha (standalone; Zerodha's standalone and consolidated revenue differ by under 0.5%), and another 10.5% on marketing. Groww also runs an asset manager, a wealth business and an NBFC. [Dhan](/dhan-fy2025) ran at a 46.6% net margin in FY2025, so Indian discount brokers converge on similar margins from very different cost bases; the [Dhan vs Zerodha](/compare/dhan-vs-zerodha) comparison covers why.

## What would change this read

- **A loan-loss charge above 40% of loan interest**, or above roughly 8% of the unsecured book, in FY2027.
- **Any loss allowance on margin funding.** It would mean collateral failed to cover a sharp market fall.
- **Payroll outgrowing revenue again** once Fisdom is in both years; it rose 26.1% on a like-for-like basis in FY2026.
