Meesho's first annual accounts as a listed Indian company come in two sets that tell opposite stories. The parent company's own accounts show the largest profit in its history. The group it controls, which is where the business now sits, lost money, and lost more of it running the business than a year earlier.

## What Each Set of Books Now Describes

[Last year's teardown](/meesho-fy2025) and the [group map](/meesho-group-map) ended on an open question: the Delaware parent, Meesho Inc, still owned 96.87% of the Indian company at March 2025. FY2026 closes it. A composite scheme approved by the company law tribunal on 27 May 2025 did three things at once:

- **Marketplace out.** The marketplace business moved from Meesho Limited to its wholly owned subsidiary Meesho Technologies Private Limited, effective 1 June 2025.
- **Grocery out.** The grocery business moved to Meesho Grocery Private Limited on the same date.
- **Delaware in.** Meesho Inc was amalgamated into Meesho Limited, accounted for as a combination of entities under common control, so no gain arises on that leg.

Meesho Limited then listed on 10 December 2025. The result is a parent that, for ten of the twelve months, no longer ran the marketplace itself.

That changes what each set of accounts can tell us. The **standalone** accounts now capture a holding company: two months of marketplace trading before the transfer, the activities that stayed with the parent, its treasury and its investments in subsidiaries. The **consolidated** accounts capture the business: buyers, sellers, logistics and advertising across every entity. Standalone revenue fell 32% to ₹6,380.92 Cr while group revenue rose 34%. The company's own results note that the two standalone years are not comparable. Every operating comparison below therefore uses the group accounts.

## Where a ₹26,376.55 Cr Gain Came From

The mechanics first. When the parent transferred the marketplace and grocery businesses, it handed over their net assets at book value. In return, the two subsidiaries issued it equity shares and compulsorily convertible preference shares, which the parent recorded at fair value. The gap between a small book value and a large fair value is booked as a gain on demerger, shown net of the costs of the business combination as an exceptional item.

The balance sheet shows where it landed. The parent's non-current investments went from ₹22.16 Cr to ₹30,611.24 Cr. Its net worth went from ₹1,516.55 Cr to ₹33,154.20 Cr. The group's net worth is ₹4,386.37 Cr. Most of the ₹28,767.83 Cr difference reflects the parent's own valuation of the businesses it moved, which the group accounts do not recognise, because a company cannot book a gain by selling to itself.

## The Loss That Shrank and the Loss That Grew

The group net loss narrowed by ₹2,583.97 Cr. Almost all of that is one-offs leaving the comparison. FY2025 carried ₹1,346.43 Cr of exceptional items (the company's results attribute these to a stock-option plan modification, accelerated vesting, perquisite tax paid on employees' behalf and merger costs) plus a ₹2,486.84 Cr tax charge, together ₹3,833.28 Cr. FY2026's equivalents were ₹141.09 Cr of exceptional items and ₹148.47 Cr of tax, together ₹289.56 Cr.

One correction to our own earlier reading: [last year's teardown](/meesho-fy2025) described the FY2025 tax charge as largely deferred. The accounts classify it as current tax, and the FY2026 results describe it as tax payable on account of the business combination, including US Global Intangible Low-Taxed Income. It was a cash obligation, and the cash section below shows it being paid.

## Why Did the Loss Before One-Offs Widen While Revenue Grew 34%?

The arithmetic is short. Revenue rose ₹3,236.45 Cr. Other expenses, which include advertising, rose ₹4,079.04 Cr. Employee costs rose just ₹64.02 Cr (7.5%), so payroll is not the story. Revenue minus other expenses swung from +₹269.68 Cr to -₹572.91 Cr.

Two lines did the work:

- **Advertising rose 74% to ₹1,119.57 Cr.** Still far below the FY2022 peak recorded in the [ten-year read](/meesho-ten-year-read), but the fastest growth in advertising since that peak.
- **The miscellaneous expense bucket rose to ₹12,032.83 Cr from ₹8,088.97 Cr.** This is the largest cost line and the summary accounts do not break it down.

What could sit inside that growth:

- **Logistics build-out.** The company's segment disclosure places logistics inside the marketplace segment and shows most of the deterioration there. A new logistics subsidiary, Valmo Transportation, was set up in January 2026.
- **Buying growth.** Higher advertising is consistent with paying to add or reactivate users ahead of the listing year's growth.
- **Low monetisation by design.** Meesho's model keeps seller fees low, so revenue can lag the costs of handling more orders.
- **New initiatives.** Reported as a separate segment; the segment figures show its loss is small next to the marketplace's.

The filing is consistent with the logistics and growth-spend explanations being the main contributors, but it does not disaggregate them. What the filing supports clearly is direction: in FY2026, other expenses grew 45% against revenue's 34%.

## An IPO In, a Tax Bill Out

The cash year has three movements.

**Operations used ₹3,875.34 Cr,** against ₹539.37 Cr generated in FY2025. The largest single cause is visible on the balance sheet: current provisions of ₹2,497.99 Cr at March 2025 fell to ₹13.38 Cr. The published cash-flow statement shows income tax paid as the biggest operating outflow, consistent with settling the business-combination tax. It also shows operations consuming cash before tax, so the tax bill explains most of the swing, not all of it.

**Financing brought in ₹4,101.38 Cr,** mainly the IPO's fresh issue net of issue costs and lease payments. **Investing was +₹247.27 Cr** as treasury holdings were drawn down: current investments fell to ₹951.78 Cr from ₹4,983.42 Cr.

The balance sheet ended stronger in capital and thinner in liquid treasury. Group net worth tripled to ₹4,386.37 Cr, with no borrowings. Cash, bank balances and current investments fell to ₹2,844.91 Cr from ₹5,361.81 Cr, while other financial assets rose (current ₹2,450.40 Cr from ₹1,366.38 Cr; non-current ₹1,858.42 Cr from ₹264.73 Cr); the summary does not say how much of that is deposits. Trade payables grew to ₹1,569.73 Cr from ₹1,070.99 Cr, the normal marketplace float that settles with sellers and logistics partners after buyers pay.

## What Remains Unresolved

- **Tax disputes.** The company has disclosed income tax demands for two earlier assessment years. It is contesting both and has a court stay on one.
- **Another reorganisation.** On 31 March 2026 the board approved moving logistics functions, staff and contracts across Meesho Technologies and Valmo Transportation. The FY2027 standalone accounts will shift again.
- **Parent profit built on subsidiaries.** Income on the subsidiaries' preference shares will keep lifting the parent's profit. It is an intra-group flow, not external earnings.
- **The cost line.** The group returns toward FY2025's near break-even only if other expenses grow slower than revenue.
