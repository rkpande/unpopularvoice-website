PhonePe spent last year proving that its cash generation could run ahead of its accounting loss. This year both measures moved the wrong way, and the reasons sit in three different places: a revenue line that stopped compounding, a cost base that kept growing, and write-downs on businesses that did not work.

[Last year's teardown](/phonepe-fy2025) argued that PhonePe's loss was mostly stock-based pay and depreciation, and that the cash line told the better story. FY2026 tests that argument. The figures below are for PhonePe Limited, the operating company, unless marked as group.

## The Operating Loss Widened by ₹836 Cr. Stock Pay Explains Little of It.

Total expenses rose 16.2% to ₹9,393.85 Cr. Total income rose 6.6% to ₹7,713.69 Cr. The gap between those two growth rates is the whole operating story.

Both readings are accurate. The statutory loss more than doubled, mostly because of write-downs. The operating result before stock pay stayed positive but shrank by more than half. That second fact is the new one, because last year's argument rested on it.

Where the ₹836.14 Cr went:

- **Advertising and promotion: +₹400.37 Cr.** Spending rose to ₹748.25 Cr from ₹347.89 Cr, from 5.3% to 10.9% of revenue. Last year's article counted the 27% fall in advertising as the headline efficiency gain. That gain has been more than given back.
- **Other operating costs: +₹404.27 Cr.** The largest unlabelled expense line rose to ₹2,827.19 Cr. The accounts do not itemise it. The prospectus presents payment processing charges as a separate, large group cost, which makes them the most likely main component.
- **Employee costs other than stock pay: +₹284.96 Cr.** They rose 20.6% to ₹1,669.77 Cr, four times the rate of revenue.
- **Offsets.** Revenue added ₹335.74 Cr and other income ₹138.52 Cr. Depreciation fell ₹32.60 Cr to ₹1,113.34 Cr.

Stock-based pay was ₹2,146.38 Cr, 31.2% of revenue. It is still the largest single reason PhonePe reports a loss. It is not the reason the loss grew.

## Why Did Revenue Growth Fall From 33% to 5%?

Revenue from services, the core line, rose 1.6% to ₹6,442.85 Cr. Other operating revenue more than doubled to ₹436.73 Cr and supplied most of the year's growth. The accounts do not break that line down. The group figure for the same line is smaller, ₹301.90 Cr, so part of the standalone amount is income from PhonePe's own subsidiaries that disappears on consolidation.

The filing does not explain the slowdown. Plausible drivers:

- **Payment categories that closed.** Public reporting around the prospectus says PhonePe exited or scaled back rent payments and real-money gaming payments after regulatory changes in 2025. Both would sit in the payments revenue that PhonePe Limited books.
- **Policy-linked income.** Part of the payments revenue depends on government and network incentives whose size is set by policy, not by PhonePe.
- **Activity moving to subsidiaries.** Lending, insurance and wealth distribution run partly through separate companies, so their growth shows in the group figures rather than here. Group revenue grew 11.3% to ₹7,920.49 Cr against ₹7,114.86 Cr originally reported for FY2025.
- **Pricing on the core rail.** UPI transfers carry no consumer fee, so transaction growth does not convert into revenue on its own.

The first two are the most consistent with the timing described in the next section. Whatever the mix, the filing supports "reported revenue slowed sharply". It does not support "PhonePe's payment volumes slowed", which the accounts do not measure.

## The Prospectus Half and the Full Year

[Last year's teardown](/phonepe-fy2025) added the April to September 2025 group figures from PhonePe's updated draft prospectus. The full-year group accounts now let the second half be derived by subtraction.

The second-half column is arithmetic, not a reported figure. The bases also differ slightly: the full-year accounts show the closed business below the line as a discontinued operation, while the prospectus half still counted it in ordinary results.

**The second half carried much less of the operating loss.** Before exceptional items, tax and stock-based pay, the first half was -₹72.20 Cr and the second half +₹262.08 Cr. The difference is mostly stock-based pay. The first half included the founder award change and early option settlements that the prospectus quantified at about ₹800 Cr (₹786 Cr plus ₹13 Cr); the second-half figure leaves no room for a charge of that size.

**Revenue did not reaccelerate.** First-half group revenue grew 22.2% on the prior-year half. On the derived figures, second-half revenue grew about 2.4% on the second half of FY2025. That is the timing that fits payment categories closing in the middle of the year.

**Cash turned positive again.** After -₹117.27 Cr in the first half, the second half generated about ₹623.36 Cr from operations.

## Why the Group Lost Less Than PhonePe Limited

The group lost ₹2,791.59 Cr. PhonePe Limited, one company inside it, lost ₹3,243.50 Cr. This usually surprises readers, so the mechanics come first.

PhonePe Limited carries its subsidiaries at what it paid for them. When a subsidiary's prospects fall, the parent writes that cost down and the write-down passes through its own profit and loss. The group accounts never held the subsidiaries at cost: they absorbed each subsidiary's losses line by line, every year. So a write-down that is large at the parent level largely does not exist at group level.

- **PhonePe Limited:** exceptional items of -₹1,563.34 Cr, net. Its non-current investments fell from ₹3,152.77 Cr to ₹2,570.40 Cr. The accounts do not split the exceptional line, so the gross write-downs may be larger if it also contains a gain.
- **Group:** exceptional items of -₹231.02 Cr. Group goodwill fell by ₹684.70 Cr, to ₹374.09 Cr, which matches the impairment of the Indus Appstore business that PhonePe disclosed publicly. The ₹434.47 Cr gain on selling part of the C.E. Info Systems (MapmyIndia) stake, booked in the first half, offsets most of it.
- **Group discontinued operation:** a separate loss of ₹364.71 Cr. Public reporting identifies it as the Pincode shopping business, which PhonePe closed.

Subsidiaries also lost money before one-offs: the group's pre-exceptional loss was ₹520.42 Cr larger than PhonePe Limited's. That residual is after intercompany eliminations, so it is not a clean measure of any one subsidiary's performance.

The FY2025 article flagged a ₹420.50 Cr write-down as a sign that "some bets are not working". FY2026 names two of them: an app store that PhonePe has described as pre-revenue, and a shopping app now closed.

## Cash: Still Positive, Thinner

Cash from operations was ₹1,374.55 Cr, 20.0% of revenue. After ₹781.24 Cr of equipment purchases and ₹207.85 Cr of lease payments, about ₹385.46 Cr remained, against ₹895.99 Cr on the same basis in FY2025. At group level, cash from operations was ₹506.09 Cr and the same calculation is negative, about -₹488.53 Cr.

The balance sheet absorbed this without strain. Cash, bank balances and current investments totalled ₹5,987.43 Cr, against ₹6,114.33 Cr a year earlier. There were no borrowings at year-end; ₹88.74 Cr was borrowed and repaid during the year. No equity was raised beyond ₹9.16 Cr from option exercises.

Equity fell only ₹253.62 Cr, to ₹11,457.12 Cr, despite the ₹3,243.50 Cr loss. Stock-based pay settled in shares is credited back to equity, so it shrinks profit without shrinking net worth. The accounts do not itemise every equity movement, and some of the increase is not explained by the stock charge alone.

## What the Second Half Settles, and What It Doesn't

The prospectus half raised a question: was the deterioration the one-off founder and option actions, or the business? The full year gives a split answer.

- **The stock charge was largely one-off.** Group stock-based pay fell to about ₹577.60 Cr in the second half.
- **The write-downs were concentrated.** They trace to an app store and a closed shopping business, not to payments.
- **The cost base was not one-off.** Advertising and non-stock staff costs grew far faster than revenue at PhonePe Limited all year.
- **Revenue is the open question.** The second-half group growth of about 2.4% is the weakest figure in either set of accounts.

PhonePe's loss can now narrow only if revenue outgrows a cost base that is still rising faster than it.
