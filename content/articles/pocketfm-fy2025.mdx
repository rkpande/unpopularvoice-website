## A ₹61 Cr OCF Swing. Then an 8x Wider Loss.

PocketFM's operating cash flow swung ₹61 Cr in FY2024.

In FY2025, revenue doubled again and the net loss grew 8x, to ₹127.26 Cr.

The FY2024 turnaround ran through one customer in the United States.

The FY2024 audited statements disclose that one unnamed customer represented 50.48% of total revenue: ₹131.68 Cr out of ₹260.85 Cr. In FY2023, the same customer (or one of similar scale) was 26.82% of revenue. The concentration doubled in one year. The US revenue line (₹131.68 Cr) now exceeds India revenue (₹129.17 Cr). The FY2024 metrics look like a company that cracked the model. The geographic disclosure shows how narrow that base was, and the FY2025 loss shows the model did not hold its FY2024 loss ratio as revenue doubled.

## The Revenue Model: Subscription Audio, Two Markets

PocketFM operates a serialised audio content platform. Listeners pay a subscription to access ongoing story serials in Hindi, regional Indian languages, and English. The business has no advertising revenue. It is a pure subscription model.

The geographic split is what makes the FY2024 filing unusual. India revenue: ₹129.17 Cr. US revenue: ₹131.68 Cr. The US is the larger market by a narrow margin, despite being a newer geography and despite PocketFM being an India-origin product.

The single-customer disclosure sits inside this geographic data. One customer in the United States represents over half the company's total revenue. This is almost certainly a platform distribution or content licensing arrangement, not a direct subscriber. The filing does not identify the customer or the nature of the relationship.

The FY2023 comparison matters: that same customer (or class of customer) went from ₹34.82 Cr to ₹131.68 Cr in one year, a 278% increase. India revenue grew 47% in the same period (₹87.82 Cr to ₹129.17 Cr). The US concentration is not stable. It is actively increasing.

## The Cost Structure and the IT Leverage Story

FY2024 total expenses: ₹277.77 Cr on ₹260.85 Cr in revenue.

The breakdown:

Employee benefit expenses: ₹111.91 Cr.
Advertising and promotion: ₹91.14 Cr.
Legal and professional fees: ₹29.25 Cr.
IT and infrastructure: ₹19.74 Cr.
Depreciation: ₹16.63 Cr.
Training and recruitment: ₹3.35 Cr.

Two observations.

First, the IT cost line is the operating leverage story. PocketFM spent ₹39.35 Cr on IT in FY2023 and ₹19.74 Cr in FY2024, while revenue grew from ₹129.75 Cr to ₹260.85 Cr. Infrastructure cost halved while revenue doubled. That ratio improvement (from 30% of revenue to 7.6%) is the primary driver of the OCF swing from -₹50.24 Cr to +₹10.91 Cr. The business is digitally native; once the infrastructure is built, incremental users cost almost nothing to serve.

Second, the legal and professional fees line (₹29.25 Cr) likely includes creator payments, content licensing, and royalty arrangements. Audio serial content requires writers and voice artists. The filing does not break this out separately. A company running ₹580 Cr in revenue in FY2025 with meaningful content volume has non-trivial creator costs, but they appear absorbed into professional fees.

## Why the FY2024 Loss Fell 77%

The FY2023 to FY2024 improvement is not a one-line story.

FY2023: Revenue ₹129.75 Cr. Net loss ₹68.71 Cr. OCF: -₹50.24 Cr.
FY2024: Revenue ₹260.85 Cr. Net loss ₹15.70 Cr. OCF: +₹10.91 Cr.

Revenue doubled. The loss fell 77%. OCF improved by ₹61 Cr.

The structural change is the IT cost halving. But there is also the revenue mix: if the US customer relationship grew from ₹34.82 Cr to ₹131.68 Cr without a proportional increase in cost, that ₹96.86 Cr of incremental high-margin revenue flows almost entirely to the bottom line. The US platform/distribution deal (if that is what it is) may be structurally more profitable than the India subscription base because the marginal cost of serving it is near zero.

This is the interpretation the filing supports but cannot confirm. What is confirmed: revenue grew faster than costs on every line except employee expenses, and the operating leverage from the IT infrastructure materialised exactly as expected for a subscription digital platform.

<InsightBox title="The Single-Customer Risk">
One unnamed US customer was 50.48% of FY2024 revenue (₹131.68 Cr). If that relationship ends or reduces materially, PocketFM's revenue base contracts to ₹129.17 Cr, identical to FY2023 levels. The cost structure is not built for ₹129 Cr in revenue: FY2025 employee costs alone were ₹241.88 Cr. A revenue contraction of that magnitude would deepen a loss that was already ₹127.26 Cr in FY2025. There is no public visibility into the terms, duration, or renewal status of this customer relationship. The concentration increased from 26.82% to 50.48% in a single year. It is the most material undisclosed risk on the page.

## FY2025: Revenue Doubled Again. The Loss Grew 8x.

FY2025 revenue was ₹580.45 Cr, up 122.5% from ₹260.85 Cr. The net loss widened from ₹15.70 Cr to ₹127.26 Cr. The loss ratio moved from 6.0% to 21.9%.

The two largest cost lines account for most of the gap. Employee benefit expense rose 116% to ₹241.88 Cr, slightly slower than revenue. Other expenses nearly tripled, from ₹149.23 Cr to ₹436.85 Cr (+193%). Together the two lines were 100% of revenue in FY2024 and 117% in FY2025.

The pattern is consistent with spending scaled ahead of revenue, with marketing, content and creator costs the usual candidates in subscription audio. The figures here do not split other expenses by line, so the driver is not confirmed.

Net worth still rose, from ₹105.24 Cr to ₹184.33 Cr (+₹79.09 Cr), despite the loss. That implies roughly ₹206 Cr of equity-side additions in the year (₹184.33 Cr less ₹105.24 Cr, plus the ₹127.26 Cr loss), such as share issuance or ESOP credits. The figures here do not split it. Equity added during the year more than covered the loss; at the FY2025 loss rate, ₹184.33 Cr of net worth is about 17 months of cover.

## One Customer, a Cost Base Ahead of Revenue and 17 Months of Cover

**The US customer concentration must be disclosed and diversified.** At 50.48% of FY2024 revenue, one customer is a single point of failure for the entire business. A credible story requires either knowing the contractual terms (multi-year, locked in) or evidence that the concentration is declining as India and other markets grow. ₹580 Cr in revenue with that customer at the same share means ₹293 Cr is still dependent on one unnamed relationship. The filing provides no comfort here.

**The cost base must stop outgrowing revenue.** The FY2024 improvement came from IT cost leverage. FY2025 shows that leverage did not extend to the wider cost base: employee and other expenses went from 100% to 117% of revenue. Bringing that ratio back down is the precondition for the loss ratio to return toward FY2024's 6%.

**The equity cushion must buy a narrower loss.** Roughly ₹206 Cr of equity-side additions lifted net worth to ₹184.33 Cr. At the FY2025 loss rate that is about 17 months of cover. The next audited year needs to show the loss ratio falling from 21.9% while revenue keeps growing.

PocketFM showed in FY2024 that the model can run near break-even at ₹261 Cr of revenue. FY2025 showed that doubling revenue did not keep it there. Whether the arc resumes depends on cost discipline and on a customer relationship that the company has not publicly named.

PocketFM didn't just find growth. It found it in one place.
