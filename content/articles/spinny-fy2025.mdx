## Two Loss Figures, One Company

Spinny's operating company lost ₹672.82 Cr in FY2025. The group it heads lost ₹423.82 Cr.

Valuedrive Technologies, the company that runs Spinny, reports two sets of audited accounts. Its standalone accounts show revenue of ₹4,650.36 Cr, a loss of ₹672.82 Cr and net worth of ₹1,496.44 Cr. Its consolidated accounts, which add four wholly-owned subsidiaries, show:

Consolidated revenue: ₹4,656.83 Cr.
Consolidated net loss: ₹423.82 Cr.
Consolidated net worth: ₹1,529.61 Cr.

Revenue barely moves on consolidation (+₹6.47 Cr). The loss moves by ₹249 Cr. The trend differs too: revenue grew about 25% on both bases, but the standalone loss widened 33.5% from ₹503.82 Cr in FY2024 while the consolidated loss narrowed 28.2% from ₹590.37 Cr.

## How the Structure Works

Valuedrive Technologies (the entity in public filings, branded as Spinny) is both the operating company and the group parent. Its standalone revenue of ₹4,650 Cr is almost all of the group's ₹4,657 Cr. Its wholly-owned subsidiaries extend the business into lending, insurance and related services.

The subsidiaries as of March 31, 2024 (the most recent disclosure with named subsidiaries):

**Northcred Services Private Limited** (100% owned): The auto NBFC. It underwrites loans for car buyers on the Spinny platform. Northcred reported revenue of ₹100.82 Cr in FY2024, up from substantially smaller numbers in prior years. The NBFC margin (net interest income minus credit losses) is the financial engine of this entity.

**Spinny Capital Private Limited** (100% owned): Likely a treasury or additional lending entity.

**Spinny Insurance Broker Private Limited** (100% owned): Insurance distribution to car buyers and sellers. A natural adjacency: anyone buying a used car needs insurance, and a platform that controls the transaction can capture that distribution.

**Quixote Automotive Technologies Private Limited** (100% owned): Its role is not separately described in the figures reviewed here.

Consolidation adds only ₹6.47 Cr of revenue after intercompany eliminations. That is net arithmetic, not a read on any one subsidiary's revenue.

## What the CSR Disclosure Shows

The corporate disclosure form exists because the Companies Act requires companies above certain scale thresholds to spend 2% of average profits on corporate social responsibility. To determine applicability, the form asks for net worth, turnover, and net profit.

Valuedrive's FY2025 corporate disclosure shows:

- Net worth: ₹14,964,439,711 = ₹1,496.44 Cr
- Turnover: ₹46,503,563,578 = ₹4,650.36 Cr
- Net Profit: -(₹6,728,176,607) = -₹672.82 Cr
- Trigger criteria: "Net Worth and Turnover"

These match the standalone audited accounts exactly: the CSR test applies to the company's own figures, not the group's.

Both the net worth (exceeding ₹500 Cr threshold) and the turnover (exceeding ₹1,000 Cr threshold) independently trigger CSR obligations. The loss means there is no 2% of profits to allocate, so CSR spending would be from other resources or deferred.

## The Economics of Used Car Selling at Scale

₹4,650 Cr in "turnover" for a used car marketplace most likely means the gross proceeds from vehicle sales, not the company's net revenue in the service-fee sense. If Spinny operates under an inventory model (buying cars, refurbishing, and reselling through the operating company), the ₹4,650 Cr is the aggregate selling price of all vehicles sold. Under a pure marketplace or commission model, ₹4,650 Cr in turnover would imply an implausibly high volume; the inventory model is the more consistent interpretation. But the filing does not explicitly state how revenue is recognised.

If the inventory model interpretation holds:
- Cost of goods (purchasing and refurbishing cars): approximately ₹4,200-4,400 Cr
- Implied gross profit: ₹250-450 Cr
- Operating expenses on top of that (people, real estate, marketing, NBFC provisioning): the remaining driver of the ₹672.82 Cr standalone loss

At average car selling prices of ₹7-10 lakh, the implied volume is approximately 46,000-66,000 vehicles sold annually. That is a significant physical operation: dozens of Spinny-branded inspection centres and car hubs, a refurbishment workforce, and delivery logistics. This is an illustrative estimate, not a disclosed figure.

The ₹672.82 Cr standalone loss on ₹4,650 Cr revenue is a 14.5% net loss ratio; at group level, ₹423.82 Cr on ₹4,657 Cr is 9.1%. For an asset-intensive used car business still expanding geographically, this reflects sustained investment in new city launches, working capital for vehicle inventory, Northcred's NBFC provisioning, and marketing to acquire buyers and sellers.

## Niraj Singh's Bet on Trust

Spinny's founder and CEO Niraj Singh built the brand around a single differentiator: trust in a market notorious for opacity. Buying a used car in India has historically meant opaque pricing, undisclosed accident history, and seller misrepresentation. Spinny's core promise is inspection transparency (every car gets a 200+ point inspection), fixed pricing (no negotiation theatre), and a return window.

That brand positioning is not accidental. Singh's insight was that the used car market was large enough to support a premium-trust brand that charged slightly more than the grey market but sold faster because buyers trusted the product. Northcred, the auto NBFC, extends the trust model to financing: buyers who want to finance their purchase do not need to find a bank separately.

The public filings confirm the scale Singh has built. ₹4,657 Cr in consolidated revenue is not a small experiment. Whether the trust positioning commands enough margin to cover the operating losses is the central question.

## Northcred: The NBFC Inside the Used Car Platform

Northcred Services Private Limited reported FY2024 revenue of ₹100.82 Cr, up from ₹11.86 Cr in FY2023.

That growth (₹11.86 Cr to ₹100.82 Cr in one year) suggests either a step-change in loan disbursements or a change in accounting as Northcred scaled its auto loan book. An NBFC earns net interest margin on the loan book: if Northcred's book grew from, say, ₹500 Cr to ₹2,000+ Cr in FY2024, the interest income would jump accordingly.

The strategic logic is sound. Platforms that control both the car transaction (Spinny) and the financing (Northcred) can capture more of the economic value from each vehicle sold. They do not need to rely on third-party banks for buyer credit and can price financing to their advantage.

The risk is symmetric: if the loan book develops high delinquency rates (used car buyers skew to sub-prime borrowers in many markets), Northcred's credit losses would flow through the consolidated P&L, worsening an already significant loss position.

## What Must Happen

Spinny does not fail because of growth. It fails if unit economics do not improve.

₹4,657 Cr in consolidated revenue is large. A ₹423.82 Cr group loss is still 27.7% of consolidated net worth in a single year, more than the business can sustain indefinitely. ₹1,530 Cr in equity gives roughly three and a half years of runway at the FY2025 group loss rate (about two years at the operating company's ₹672.82 Cr standalone loss). The question is whether the loss rate is compressing fast enough.

Three things determine the answer.

**Vehicle gross margins:** If the per-car margin (selling price minus purchase cost minus refurbishment minus delivery) is expanding as Spinny scales, the fixed overhead becomes increasingly covered by a growing gross profit pool. If margins remain thin (as they are in most used car markets globally), volume growth does not solve the problem.

**Northcred credit quality:** The NBFC tripled revenue in FY2024. Loan quality on those books will reveal itself in FY2025 and FY2026 through provisioning. If Northcred's borrowers default at elevated rates, consolidated losses worsen at exactly the moment vehicle economics need to be improving.

**Loss trajectory year-over-year:** The consolidated loss narrowed from ₹590.37 Cr in FY2024 to ₹423.82 Cr in FY2025 while revenue grew about 25%. The standalone loss moved the other way, widening from ₹503.82 Cr to ₹672.82 Cr. Which of the two trends continues is the number that would most change this analysis.

<InsightBox title="Why Standalone and Consolidated Diverge">
Valuedrive Technologies is both the operating company and the parent of four subsidiaries, so it reports two sets of accounts. Revenue is almost identical across them (₹4,650.36 Cr standalone, ₹4,656.83 Cr consolidated). The loss is not (₹672.82 Cr standalone, ₹423.82 Cr consolidated). Intercompany transactions are eliminated on consolidation, and items booked only in the parent's own accounts, such as provisions against its investments in or loans to subsidiaries, can reverse. The figures reviewed here do not show which of these drives the ₹249 Cr gap. Any single Spinny loss figure needs its perimeter stated alongside it.
