The improvement came from the cost side: what it lost to bad loans and what it spent running the book both fell faster than revenue. The notes add two details the headline lines do not show, about where credit risk is building and who is funding the book.

## Akara Capital Advisors: A Wholly Owned Lender

- **Legal entity**: Akara Capital Advisors Private Limited, incorporated in Delhi in 2016. Stashfin is the brand.
- **Business**: an RBI-registered NBFC lending to individuals. Of ₹1,742.25 Cr in gross loans, ₹1,741.71 Cr is unsecured.
- **Owner**: a wholly owned subsidiary of Morus Technologies Pte Ltd.
- **Accounts**: signed by Tushar Aggarwal (Managing Director) and Shruti Aggarwal (Director); audited by Serva Associates, 28 May 2025.

The standalone accounts capture the whole lending book and its funding. They do not show the parent's accounts, so the parent loan and stock option plan are visible only from this side.

## Why Did Profit Rise While Revenue Fell?

Start with revenue. Interest income rose 7.7% to ₹475.18 Cr as the book grew. Fees and commission income fell 31.8%, from ₹343.41 Cr to ₹234.30 Cr, and its share of revenue fell from 43.8% to 33.0%. The accounts report fees as one line, "service and other fees", so they do not show which fees fell. FY2025 is the first revenue decline in the four years of accounts on record; FY2024 revenue was 3.7 times FY2023's ₹213.15 Cr.

Now the bridge from FY2024 pre-tax profit to FY2025, line by line:

| Line | FY2024 | FY2025 | Effect on pre-tax profit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue from operations | ₹784.63 Cr | ₹709.64 Cr | -₹74.99 Cr |
| Other income | ₹0.42 Cr | ₹0.06 Cr | -₹0.36 Cr |
| Impairment on financial instruments | ₹403.81 Cr | ₹326.58 Cr | +₹77.23 Cr |
| Other expenses | ₹112.64 Cr | ₹72.96 Cr | +₹39.68 Cr |
| Depreciation and amortisation | ₹10.47 Cr | ₹3.38 Cr | +₹7.09 Cr |
| Finance costs | ₹149.85 Cr | ₹147.99 Cr | +₹1.86 Cr |
| Fair value loss | ₹0.86 Cr | nil | +₹0.86 Cr |
| Employee benefits | ₹35.30 Cr | ₹41.46 Cr | -₹6.16 Cr |
| **Profit before tax** | **₹72.12 Cr** | **₹117.34 Cr** | **+₹45.22 Cr** |

Rows are rounded and may not add to the last paisa.

Two lines carry the year. The impairment charge has two parts in the notes:

- **Write-offs, net of recoveries**: ₹318.52 Cr, down from ₹349.45 Cr, a ₹30.93 Cr improvement. That is about 19.5% of average gross loans.
- **Provision build**: ₹8.05 Cr, down 85.2% from ₹54.36 Cr, a ₹46.30 Cr improvement.

Other expenses fell mainly through one undefined line, "direct expense", down from ₹96.02 Cr to ₹50.60 Cr. One plausible reading is that costs tied to fee-earning activity shrank with fee income; the accounts do not link the two.

Credit costs took ₹46 of every ₹100 of revenue, against ₹51 in FY2024.

Profit after tax rose more slowly than pre-tax profit, 23.3% against 62.7%. FY2024's tax charge was only ₹3.30 Cr because of a ₹29.00 Cr deferred tax credit. FY2025's charge was ₹32.49 Cr.

## Stage 2 Loans Rose 72%. The Provision Build Fell 85%.

The staging note sorts the gross book (excluding accrued interest) into the three ECL buckets:

| Stage | Gross FY2024 | Gross FY2025 | Allowance FY2024 | Allowance FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stage 1 (performing) | ₹1,229.48 Cr | ₹1,278.02 Cr | ₹24.80 Cr | ₹9.11 Cr |
| Stage 2 (higher risk) | ₹168.58 Cr | ₹289.47 Cr | ₹31.95 Cr | ₹41.43 Cr |
| Stage 3 (impaired) | ₹73.23 Cr | ₹74.78 Cr | ₹40.75 Cr | ₹48.72 Cr |
| **Total** | **₹1,471.29 Cr** | **₹1,642.27 Cr** | **₹97.50 Cr** | **₹99.26 Cr** |

The staged book grew 11.6%. Stage 2 grew 71.7% and rose from 11.5% to 17.6% of the book. Stage 3 fell from 5.0% to 4.6%, and the allowance against it rose from 55.6% to 65.2% of the balance.

The total allowance moved by ₹1.76 Cr, all of it booked on one line, "ECL remeasurement due to changes in EAD, credit risk and assumptions". The same line added ₹50.04 Cr in FY2024. By stage, the Stage 1 allowance fell 63.3%, and its coverage fell from 2.02% to 0.71%. (The ₹8.05 Cr provision in the profit and loss also covers first-loss guarantee recoverables, which this table excludes.)

Three readings fit these numbers:

- **Revised assumptions.** Lower Stage 1 loss rates would cut that allowance with no change in borrower behaviour.
- **Better recent loans.** Fewer defaults on newer loans would feed into lower Stage 1 rates.
- **Faster migration.** ₹587.90 Cr moved from Stage 1 to Stage 2 in the year, leaving a lower-risk Stage 1 behind.

The accounts show the direction: a much larger Stage 2, a much smaller Stage 1 provision, and a total allowance that barely moved. They do not say which reading dominates.

Borrowings rose ₹194.88 Cr. The parent loan added ₹153.92 Cr and debt securities ₹139.97 Cr, while term loans from other lenders fell ₹157.63 Cr. The parent's share of borrowings rose from 42.8% to 49.4%. Borrowings from outside India were ₹554.39 Cr, up from ₹385.12 Cr. Corporate guarantees from group companies on Stashfin's loans fell to ₹213.04 Cr from ₹420.83 Cr.

Finance costs fell even though debt grew. Interest on the parent loan rose 79% to ₹47.17 Cr, and foreign exchange loss rose to ₹13.48 Cr from ₹7.89 Cr, while interest on debentures fell to ₹25.64 Cr from ₹42.89 Cr. Year-end balances do not show average debt, so lender rates are approximate: on a simple average, the parent loan cost about 10.4% before currency losses.

Borrowings are 0.65 times net loans (FY2024: 0.62) and 1.55 times net worth (1.48).

Net worth rose ₹99.82 Cr with no new shares. Profit after tax added ₹84.84 Cr. A further ₹14.40 Cr is the share-based payments reserve for stock options the parent granted to Stashfin staff, charged in employee costs. A ₹0.53 Cr tax benefit and ₹0.04 Cr of other comprehensive income make up the rest. The ₹17.08 Cr statutory reserve transfer RBI rules require stays inside net worth.

Employee costs rose 17.5% to ₹41.46 Cr. Salaries and wages more than doubled, from ₹12.27 Cr to ₹25.24 Cr, while the stock option charge fell from ₹21.80 Cr to ₹14.40 Cr.

## Four Lines the Next Accounts Will Settle

- **Stage 2.** Whether the ₹289.47 Cr in Stage 2 recovers or moves into Stage 3 and write-offs.
- **The provision build.** Whether a ₹8.05 Cr build holds while the book keeps growing.
- **Fee income.** Whether the 31.8% fall was a one-year reset or a continuing decline.
- **Parent funding.** Whether the parent keeps supplying half the debt, and at what currency cost.

The FY2025 profit holds only if the larger Stage 2 book performs the way the smaller provision implies.
