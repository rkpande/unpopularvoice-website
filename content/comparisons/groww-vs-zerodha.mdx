Zerodha earned ₹37.4 of every ₹100 of revenue from interest and returns on money it holds. Groww earned ₹8.2 that way inside revenue. Its own treasury income, ₹151.69 Cr, is booked below revenue as other income; adding it takes Groww to about ₹12.

## Which is more profitable, Groww or Zerodha?

On reported figures, Zerodha by about a point: 47.9% of FY2025 revenue ended as profit, against 46.8% at Groww (both consolidated). Before tax the margins were 63.6% and 63.1%.

Three differences sit inside those reported margins:

- **Investment returns land in different places.** Zerodha counts ₹933.31 Cr of returns on its own investments as revenue: fair-value gains of ₹814.91 Cr, interest of ₹79.78 Cr and dividends of ₹38.62 Cr. Groww books its treasury income, mostly interest on bonds and gains on mutual funds, as other income (₹151.69 Cr of its ₹159.92 Cr).
- **Groww's FY2025 carried a one-off.** Staff costs were cut by a ₹106.17 Cr reversal of a cancelled management incentive plan.
- **Exchange charges may be counted differently.** Zerodha shows ₹1,608.14 Cr of exchange and depository charges as a cost, and its stated policy counts charges recovered from clients as fee income. Groww's transaction-charges line was ₹129.55 Cr. Neither set of accounts says whether such recoveries are shown gross or net. If Zerodha grosses up what Groww nets off, Zerodha's margin on comparable revenue is higher than reported.

Remove other income and investment returns from both, and the pre-tax margin on the remaining revenue was 59.0% at Groww and 59.1% at Zerodha.

<SignatureLine text="Take out investment returns and other income, and both brokers kept about ₹59 of every ₹100 before tax." />

That near-match needs two qualifications. Groww's figure includes the one-off; without it, Groww kept ₹56.3. And Zerodha's deposit interest includes interest on its own cash, which the accounts do not separate.

A rough estimate: Zerodha held about ₹33,243 Cr in bank deposits at March 2025 against ₹25,366.40 Cr of trade payables, leaving roughly ₹7,900 Cr that is plausibly its own. At the 7.4% its deposits earned on average, that is about ₹580 Cr of interest; removing it takes Zerodha to about 56%. On the own-cash point the two stay close. The exchange-charge question could pull Zerodha well ahead, and the accounts settle neither.

Groww also spent ₹11.30 per ₹100 on software, servers and technology, and ₹8.08 on staff (₹10.80 without the reversal). Zerodha spent ₹5.41 on technology fees and ₹6.24 on staff; its largest cost was exchange and depository charges, ₹1,608.14 Cr or ₹18.18 per ₹100.

## How does Groww make money compared with Zerodha?

85.5% of Groww's FY2025 revenue was fees and commission. At Zerodha the figure was 61.2%: brokerage of ₹3,454.15 Cr plus ₹1,963.19 Cr of other commission and charges.

Groww's second line was lending: ₹245.47 Cr of interest on margin funding and personal loans. Zerodha's was interest on bank deposits: ₹2,372.84 Cr, about 4.2x everything Groww earned outside fees.

## Why does Zerodha earn so much on money it holds?

The accounts point to four contributors:

- **More client money.** Trade payables, which at a broker are mostly money owed to clients, were ₹25,366.40 Cr at Zerodha against ₹4,562.40 Cr at Groww: 5.6x, on 2.3x the revenue.
- **More of its own money.** Zerodha's net worth was ₹16,644.90 Cr, 3.4x Groww's, built from retained profit. ₹7,151.13 Cr sat in investments.
- **A different accounting home.** Zerodha's investment returns count as revenue; Groww's sit in other income.
- **Groww puts its capital into loans.** Its loan book was ₹1,690.70 Cr at March 2025. Zerodha's margin-funding book started only in FY2025.

The accounts do not split Zerodha's deposit interest between client money and its own funds. The direction is clear; the shares are not.

## Which is bigger, Groww or Zerodha?

Zerodha, by 2.3x on FY2025 revenue (₹8,846.95 Cr against ₹3,901.72 Cr) and on profit (₹4,236.72 Cr against ₹1,824.37 Cr), and by 3.4x on net worth. Take Zerodha's investment returns out of its revenue and the gap narrows to 2.0x.

Groww's FY2026 results (Zerodha's FY2026 accounts were not available for this comparison) show revenue of ₹4,644.58 Cr and profit of ₹2,083.00 Cr ([Groww FY2026](/groww-fy2026)). Those are 52.5% and 49.2% of Zerodha's FY2025 figures. The years differ, so this measures scale, not who grew faster.

## Is Groww riskier than Zerodha?

At March 2025 Groww carried ₹1,136.52 Cr of unsecured loans, mainly personal loans through its lending subsidiary. Zerodha had none beyond ₹16.61 Cr of loans to employees.

- **Credit.** Groww's loan-loss charge was ₹67.46 Cr in FY2025, 34% of its ₹197.49 Cr of loan interest, and it wrote off ₹32.97 Cr. Its loss allowance at year-end was ₹47.71 Cr, 4.2% of the unsecured book.
- **Margin funding is not the difference.** Zerodha's book, started in FY2025, was ₹851.64 Cr at year-end, larger than Groww's ₹601.89 Cr. Both carry a nil loss allowance.
- **Zerodha's exposure is its own portfolio.** It held ₹4,845.31 Cr in shares and exchange-traded funds, 29.1% of net worth. In FY2025 its fair-value gains fell ₹902.86 Cr, almost as much as its fees fell (₹977.98 Cr), while interest income rose ₹735.01 Cr.
- **Capital.** Groww raised ₹409.75 Cr of equity in FY2025 and ended the year with ₹544.36 Cr of borrowings, 0.11x its net worth. Zerodha issued no shares and had ₹0.94 Cr of borrowings.

Cash from operations does not settle it. Groww records changes in client bank balances inside operating cash flow; Zerodha records them under investing. That is part of why Groww shows -₹962.16 Cr and Zerodha +₹12,533.11 Cr, offset by -₹12,588.56 Cr of investing outflows. Groww's figure also absorbs ₹1,041.22 Cr of loan growth and ₹1,905.44 Cr of tax paid, about three times its current tax charge for the year; the accounts do not break the payment down.

## What would change this comparison

- **Zerodha's FY2026 accounts**, which would put both in the same year again. Groww's FY2026 margin was 44.8%.
- **A split of Zerodha's ₹2,372.84 Cr of deposit interest** between client money and its own funds.
- **Disclosure of how each books exchange charges**, gross or net of client recoveries.
