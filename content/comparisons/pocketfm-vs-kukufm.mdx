## FY26 UPDATE (added 9 November 2026)

The original comparison below was published in May 2026 based on the FY25 audits. The FY26 picture has changed materially. This update sits at the top; the original FY25 analysis follows for the editorial record.

**KukuFM (now Kuku Technologies Limited, converted from Mebigo Labs Private Limited)**

- FY26 revenue from operations: **₹1,484.2 Cr** (up approximately 6x from the restated FY25 figure of ₹241.6 Cr)
- FY26 net profit: **+₹182.7 Cr** (flipped from a FY25 net loss of ₹152.6 Cr)
- FY26 marketing and advertising spend: ₹1,105 Cr (up 288% from ₹285 Cr)
- FY26 employee cost: ₹74 Cr (up 24%)
- FY26 total expenditure: ₹1,422 Cr (up 3.5x)
- Subscription revenue: 99% of total; India 99% of revenue
- Confidential DRHP filed with SEBI in **June 2026**; SEBI observation letter received **September 2026**
- Target issue size: ₹2,500-3,500 Cr; target valuation ~₹15,000 Cr (approximately 10x revenue)
- 400M+ app installs, 10M+ subscribers (June 2026 disclosure)
- CIN now `U74999MH2018PLC312866` (public limited), reflecting the IPO conversion

**PocketFM (India entity: Pocket Entertainment Ventures Private Limited; US parent)**

- FY26 India-standalone audit **not yet on public register**. Group-level figures via company disclosure and press.
- Group revenue FY25: ₹1,768 Cr (up 68% from ₹1,052 Cr FY24; includes US)
- Group loss FY25: ~₹165 Cr
- September 2026 ARR: **$500M** (~₹4,200 Cr annualised, group level)
- **EBITDA profitable** at ~5% margin; free cash flow positive
- Status: **exploring reverse flip** from US to India to enable Indian IPO path; no DRHP filed; management publicly says IPO is 24 months away
- Exploring fresh funding round of ~$150M
- Entity renamed from Pocket FM Private Limited to Pocket Entertainment Ventures Private Limited; migrated from Haryana to Karnataka

### What changed inside KukuFM between FY25 and FY26

Three mechanisms are visible in the FY26 income statement that were not visible in FY25.

The first is **marketing payback**. In FY25, KukuFM spent ₹285 Cr on marketing against ₹242 Cr of revenue, a marketing-to-revenue ratio of 118%, which is the arithmetic of a company spending more to acquire customers than those customers would pay in subscription that year. In FY26, marketing spend quadrupled to ₹1,105 Cr but revenue grew 6x to ₹1,484 Cr. Marketing-to-revenue dropped to 74%. The cohort of subscribers acquired in late FY25 and early FY26 crossed the 12-month payback threshold inside the fiscal year, producing enough recurring subscription cash to cover the acquisition spend and leave a surplus.

The second is **AI content cost collapse**. KukuFM has publicly stated that AI now powers the majority of its microdrama production. Audio microdrama (short-form serialised episodes) was the fastest-growing format in Indian audio through 2026. Voice-cloning and AI-assisted script generation reduces the per-episode content cost by an order of magnitude relative to human voice-actor production. KukuFM's FY26 operational cost line of ₹96 Cr (up from ₹9 Cr in FY25) is small in absolute terms given the revenue scale, suggesting the cost of content production did not scale linearly with content output.

The third is **category concentration**. Subscription revenue was 99% of FY26 revenue, up from an already-high proportion in FY25. KukuFM did not diversify into advertising, licensing, or merchandise. The single-product focus meant every incremental acquired subscriber added directly to the subscription top line without dilution across lower-margin product lines.

### What changed in PocketFM's trajectory

PocketFM remained EBITDA profitable at the group level through calendar 2026 and grew ARR from approximately $400M in April to $500M in September. The structural picture is different from KukuFM's in two important ways.

PocketFM's revenue base is US-heavy. The FY25 standalone-India figure of ₹580 Cr sits inside a group revenue of ₹1,768 Cr, implying the US business and other geographies contributed approximately ₹1,188 Cr, roughly 67% of the global pie. This geographic concentration is the mirror image of KukuFM's India-only playbook. The US market pays more per subscriber but is also more competitive and more exposed to platform-store policy risk (Apple and Google in-app subscription rules).

PocketFM's legal structure has blocked the Indian IPO path. With the parent entity in the US, PocketFM cannot list directly on Indian exchanges. The reverse flip under exploration would collapse the US holding company into the India entity, mirroring the Flipkart and PhonePe reverse-flip playbooks. That process typically takes 10-15 months from decision to NCLT sanction; PocketFM's public timeline of "IPO in 24 months" is consistent with this duration plus a cooling-off period for SEBI readiness.

### The honest read on the May 2026 article

The May 2026 UV piece correctly recorded the FY25 balance-sheet position: KukuFM's net worth had halved, there was no fresh equity raise disclosed, and the implied burn rate pointed to less than 12 months of runway at that moment. The arithmetic was accurate for the state the company was in on 31 March 2025.

What the May 2026 piece did not model was the possibility of a **mechanism change inside FY26**: specifically, the marketing-cohort payback window closing and the AI content cost collapsing fast enough to flip the P&L. Both futures were possible from the FY25 data. The audit alone could not distinguish them. UV wrote the pessimistic read with high conviction when it should have been written with calibration between the two possible outcomes.

That is a lesson carried forward into other loss-making subscription-platform analyses on UV.

## The 30-Second Summary (on FY25 audits)

Two Indian audio platforms. Same subscription format. Same vintage.

By FY2025 they're not the same business.

- **PocketFM:** Revenue ₹580 Cr. Loss widened to ₹127 Cr, 22% of revenue. Net worth growing on equity additions.
- **KukuFM:** Revenue ₹242 Cr. Loss widening. Cash burning. Net worth more than halved.

PocketFM found a customer.

KukuFM needs a round.
