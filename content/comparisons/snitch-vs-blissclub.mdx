## The 30-Second Summary

Two Indian D2C apparel brands. Same FY2025 audit period. The same three cost lines moved in structurally opposite directions.

- **Snitch (Snitch Apparels Pvt Ltd):** revenue ₹498 Cr (+106%); **payroll ₹65 Cr (+270%)**, advertising ₹83 Cr (+136%); net result flipped from +₹4.4 Cr profit to -₹1.7 Cr loss. Zero long-term debt, net worth ₹120 Cr, cash ₹66 Cr.

- **BlissClub (Blissclub Fitness Pvt Ltd):** revenue ₹132 Cr (+51%); **payroll ₹18 Cr (-43%, a ₹13.55 Cr cut)**, advertising ₹30 Cr (+31%); net loss cut from ₹44 Cr to ₹20 Cr. Total borrowings ₹21 Cr, net worth ₹39 Cr, cash and bank ₹39 Cr (after redeeming ₹29 Cr of current investments).

One brand was at a stage to buy growth. The other was at a stage to compress costs. The audits show the decision; each playbook fits the entity's capital position and revenue scale. They are not equally available choices.

## The Category Context: Indian D2C Apparel in FY25

Both brands operate inside a category that had a specific shape in FY25, and the two audits read differently if you hold that context alongside the numbers.

Indian D2C apparel, through FY24 and into FY25, moved from a "scale at any cost" posture to a "prove the unit economics" posture. The category saw a wave of scale-stage brands either compress losses sharply (Mamaearth-adjacent beauty D2Cs, some of the pet and home D2Cs) or stall revenue growth as acquisition costs climbed faster than lifetime value. The audits that landed through the first half of calendar 2026 reflected both pressures: investors had shifted the funding ask from "faster growth" to "less burn," and the brands that could show the second got better conversations.

Snitch and BlissClub sit on two different sides of that transition. Snitch is the brand that still has permission to grow fast because the top line is still doubling and the capital base has room to absorb the cost of that growth. BlissClub is the brand that has moved into the second posture because the top-line pace is slower and the capital base is tighter.

The two cost-side choices in FY25 reflect this.

Snitch's +270% payroll growth and +136% advertising growth are the moves of a brand that chose to use its capital runway to push further into both digital customer acquisition and physical store expansion. The audit's balance-sheet signals support the physical-retail read: other long-term liabilities grew approximately 8x, contingent liabilities moved from zero to ₹24 Cr, and depreciation ran at 5.8x the prior year. All three are consistent with a company signing multi-year lease commitments and capitalising store-fitout costs. A brand expanding into physical retail hires store managers, visual merchandisers, and regional operations staff; the payroll triples because the operating model doubles its scope, not because the digital brand's team size tripled.

BlissClub's -43% payroll cut and +31% advertising growth read differently. The entity kept the customer-acquisition line in growth mode but reshaped the operating team, which likely captures some combination of headcount reductions, salary-mix changes toward less-senior roles, and ESOP accounting movements that run through employee benefit expense. The decision pattern fits a brand focused on same-store (same-channel) sales scaling rather than channel expansion, with the goal of showing operating leverage on an unchanged operating footprint.

Neither posture is right or wrong in the abstract. Both are internally consistent with the capital position they sit in. What the audits show is the decision; what they do not show is whether the decision produces the right FY26 outcome for either brand.

Snitch's three cost-side movements (employee +₹48 Cr, advertising +₹48 Cr, commission +₹13 Cr) added ₹108 Cr of cost against ₹257 Cr of revenue growth. The combined ratio of these three lines moved from 21.8% of revenue (FY24) to 32.3% (FY25), a 10.5 percentage-point operating-margin compression. The doubled cost base is the mechanism that explains the profit flip.

BlissClub moved in the opposite direction on the dominant line. Employee benefits fell ₹13.55 Cr on a 51% revenue-growth year, an unusual combination for a D2C brand at this stage. Advertising grew but stayed disciplined relative to revenue. The combined operating-overhead movement (employee + advertising + other expenses) was +₹6.46 Cr against ₹44.69 Cr of revenue growth, producing the bulk of the ₹23.75 Cr loss compression.

Two brands moved in opposite directions on the employee-benefit line during FY25. They arrived at nearly the same labour-cost intensity. Snitch added revenue faster than it added team; BlissClub cut team faster than it added revenue. The trajectories differ; the destination is approximately the same.

The audits do not disclose headcount, salary-mix, or function-level breakdown for either entity; the ratio is a year-end snapshot, not a structural conclusion. But the convergence is the kind of finding that the surface contrast hides.

<InsightBox label="The structural read" title="Each playbook fits the entity's stage; both can be internally consistent">

Tripling payroll and doubling advertising in a year when revenue doubles is a "buying growth" decision that an under-funded entity could not afford to make. The bet is that the doubled cost base produces operating leverage as revenue scales further in FY26.

BlissClub at ₹132 Cr revenue, ₹39 Cr net worth, ₹21 Cr of borrowings, and ₹29 Cr of current investments fully redeemed during FY25 has structurally less room. Cutting payroll 43% while keeping advertising in growth mode is a "managing burn" decision aimed at extending runway and producing P&L credibility for the next financing conversation. The bet is that the smaller team can support the larger revenue base without slowing execution.

## The Mechanism: How Each Playbook Actually Works

Both playbooks have an internal logic that the audit numbers alone do not fully surface. Reading the two together reveals the mechanism behind each.

**The Snitch mechanism: operating leverage from channel expansion.**

Snitch's FY25 investment is sized against a specific operating thesis. Opening physical stores (which the balance-sheet signals support) converts a digital brand into a hybrid-channel brand. Hybrid channels produce higher gross merchandise value per customer because the in-store customer typically buys more items per visit at higher average order values. In-store presence also lifts digital conversion in the brand's geographies through the brand-visibility effect.

For this thesis to work, the FY26 audit needs to show three things: revenue growth sustained above 50% (indicating the new stores are productive), gross margin either held or expanded (indicating pricing power in physical retail is not weaker than digital), and operating cost growth decelerating sharply (indicating the FY25 payroll ramp was front-loaded rather than ongoing). If all three show, the FY25 profit flip is a one-year effect that reverses in FY26. If any of them do not show, the question of whether Snitch over-invested becomes a real one.

The capital position gives Snitch the room to run this experiment for one or two more years. Zero long-term debt, ₹66 Cr of cash, and ₹120 Cr of net worth against a FY25 loss of ₹1.7 Cr implies many years of operating runway at the current burn. The risk is not survival; the risk is efficiency of capital use.

**The BlissClub mechanism: operating leverage from team compression.**

BlissClub's FY25 cut is sized against a different thesis. If the business can sustain its revenue growth with a smaller team, the operating-margin profile improves materially even without new investment. The 43% employee cost cut, if sustainable alongside continued revenue growth, produces a structural improvement in the loss ratio from 50% of revenue in FY24 to 15% in FY25, approaching break-even inside two more growth years.

For this thesis to work, the FY26 audit needs to show three different things: revenue growth sustained above 40% (indicating the smaller team can still drive the top line), employee cost growing slower than revenue (indicating the cut was a structural reset, not a one-year freeze), and OCF turning materially positive (indicating the halved P&L loss finally shows up in cash). The P&L improvement already landed in FY25; the OCF conversion is what the market will watch next.

The capital position gives BlissClub less room to run this experiment. Net worth fell ₹18 Cr during FY25, the entire ₹29 Cr of current investments was redeemed into cash, and total borrowings of ₹21 Cr already sit on the balance sheet. Approximately two more years of this burn rate without a fresh equity round. The strategic pressure to make the cost cut translate into real cash improvement in FY26 is higher at BlissClub than the pressure on any single line item is at Snitch.

**The comparative read.**

Both brands have correctly chosen the playbook their capital structure allows. Each needs a specific set of three FY26 outcomes to validate the FY25 decision. The audit is not a full test of the strategy; it is a snapshot of the mid-point. The next audit is the test.

## What to Watch in the FY26 Audits

FY26 audits for both entities are due to be filed in October 2026 for fiscal years ending 31 March 2026. The audits had not landed on the public register as of early November 2026. When they do, here is what each should be read for.

**Snitch FY26, the three lines that matter:**

Revenue trajectory. If the top line continues at 50% plus, the physical-retail investment is producing returns. If it falls to 25% to 40%, the thesis is partially validated but with meaningful compression. If it drops below 25%, the FY25 cost ramp was not matched by productive channel expansion and the question becomes whether the new team and store base can be absorbed into a slower growth rate.

Operating expense growth versus revenue growth. If operating costs grow slower than revenue in FY26, FY25 was a front-loaded investment year and operating leverage is now visible. If operating costs grow at the same rate as revenue, the cost ramp is ongoing and the profit flip of FY25 extends into FY26.

Store-count disclosure. The audit itself may not disclose store count directly, but the depreciation line, other long-term liabilities (capitalised lease commitments), and property, plant and equipment movement will indicate the pace of physical-retail expansion. A continued doubling signals aggressive expansion; a slowdown signals a pause for consolidation.

**BlissClub FY26, the three lines that matter:**

Operating cash flow. The FY25 audit showed the halved P&L loss did not translate into improved cash. The FY26 OCF is the key test. If OCF turns to meaningful positive (say +₹20 Cr or better), the P&L improvement was real and funded by operating efficiency. If OCF stays negative or improves only modestly, the P&L improvement was accounting-driven rather than cash-driven, and the strategic read on BlissClub materially changes.

Employee cost trajectory. If the FY25 cut was a one-year adjustment, the FY26 employee cost will be substantially higher than ₹18 Cr. If the cut was structural, it stays near or below that level while revenue continues to grow. The second case is much more valuable because it signals a durable operating-leverage shift.

Fresh equity raise. If BlissClub raises a new equity round during FY26 or early FY27, the capital pressure eases and the FY25 cost-cut becomes a story of discipline-driven resilience. If no raise happens and the OCF stays negative, the entity moves into true funding-pressure territory within the twelve months following the FY26 audit.

<InsightBox label="The honest editorial read" title="FY25 is not the end of the story for either brand">
Both audits capture a mid-course decision, not an endpoint. Snitch chose to invest; BlissClub chose to discipline. Both decisions are internally consistent with the capital and revenue stage of each entity. Neither decision is yet proven out in the data.

The FY26 audits will tell. The question for Snitch is whether the doubled cost base produces operating leverage as revenue scales further. The question for BlissClub is whether the cost cut translates into real cash improvement and the smaller team continues to support revenue growth.

Until then, the two audits document a choice and its arithmetic. They do not document the conclusion.
</InsightBox>
